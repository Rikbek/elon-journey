Chapter 01

A Mind of His Own

Pretoria, South Africa · 1971–1988

Elon Reeve Musk was born on June 28, 1971, in Pretoria, South Africa, to Maye Musk — a Canadian-born model and dietitian — and Errol Musk, a South African electromechanical engineer and property developer. From the earliest years, he was different. While other children played outside, Elon disappeared into books and daydreams. He would later describe himself as a quiet, intense child who preferred the company of encyclopedias and science-fiction novels.

His parents divorced when he was young. Elon lived primarily with his father — a relationship that was complex and often difficult. At school he faced relentless bullying. One attack left him so badly beaten that he was hospitalized for a week. The physical pain faded; the emotional scars remained. Yet instead of breaking, the boy turned inward and upward — into code, rockets, and the stars.

At age twelve, Elon taught himself to program on a Commodore VIC-20. He wrote a simple space-themed video game called Blastar and sold the source code to a computer magazine for $500. It was a small sum, but it proved something fundamental: ideas could become products, and products could create freedom.

Key moment: At 12 years old, Elon sold his first creation. The same boy who was beaten in the schoolyard had already begun building the future.

By his late teens, South Africa under apartheid offered limited horizons and the certainty of military conscription. Elon wanted none of it. He wanted the largest possible canvas — the United States — and the emerging internet. In 1989, using his mother’s Canadian citizenship, the seventeen-year-old left South Africa alone with a few thousand dollars and a one-way ticket toward the unknown.

Chapter 02

Escape Velocity

Canada & the United States · 1989–1995

Elon arrived in Canada with almost nothing. He worked odd jobs — cleaning boilers, cutting logs, farming — while searching for relatives he had never met. In 1990 he enrolled at Queen’s University in Kingston, Ontario. Two years later he transferred to the University of Pennsylvania, where he earned dual bachelor’s degrees: one in physics and one in economics from the Wharton School.

He was already thinking far beyond graduation. In a senior paper titled “The Importance of Being Solar,” he explored sustainable energy. He read voraciously about space, the internet, and the limits of human civilization. After finishing at Penn, he was accepted into a PhD program in materials science at Stanford University. He stayed for two days.

The year was 1995. The World Wide Web was exploding. Elon decided that the internet would change society faster than any physics research. He walked away from the doctorate, moved into a tiny office in Silicon Valley with his brother Kimbal, and prepared to build.

Turning point: Leaving Stanford after 48 hours. Most people would call it quitting. Elon called it prioritizing the highest-leverage problem of the decade.
Chapter 03

Zip2 — The First Empire

Palo Alto · 1995–1999

In 1995, Elon, Kimbal, and a friend founded Zip2 — a company that provided online city guides, maps, and business directories for newspapers. At a time when most media companies still treated the internet as a curiosity, Zip2 gave them a digital presence. Major clients included The New York Times and the Chicago Tribune.

The brothers worked brutal hours. Elon coded through the night and slept on the office floor. Money was tight; they showered at the local YMCA. Venture capital eventually arrived, but with it came pressure and boardroom politics. Elon fought to keep control of the vision even as professional managers were brought in.

In 1999, Compaq Computer acquired Zip2 for approximately $307 million in cash. Elon’s share made him a multimillionaire at age 28. Most people would have retired or slowed down. Elon did the opposite. He took the capital and immediately started the next company.

Lesson: The first exit was not the end of the story. It was the fuel for everything that followed.
Chapter 04

X.com to PayPal

1999–2002

With roughly $10–12 million of his Zip2 proceeds, Elon founded X.com in March 1999 — an ambitious online bank and financial services platform. The vision was radical: money should move as easily as email. The company grew quickly but faced fierce competition from a startup called Confinity, which had built a popular person-to-person payment tool named PayPal.

In March 2000 the two companies merged. The combined entity eventually adopted the name PayPal. Elon served as CEO, but internal conflicts over product direction and leadership style led to a boardroom coup in September 2000. He was replaced as CEO, though he remained the largest shareholder.

PayPal went public in early 2002 and was acquired by eBay later that year for $1.5 billion in stock. Elon’s personal stake was worth approximately $165–180 million after taxes. At 31 years old, he now had the capital and the freedom to attack problems that no one else was willing to touch.
